The question

Let $A$ be a representation-finite finite-dimensional algebra. Count quotient-closed and submodule-closed additive subcategories by the number of indecomposable isomorphism classes they contain. If $q_i(A)$ and $s_i(A)$ are those two counts at level $i$, the strong quotient-submodule equidistribution conjecture asks whether

$$Q_A(t)=\sum_i q_i(A)t^i \quad = \quad \sum_i s_i(A)t^i=S_A(t).$$

The formalization models right $A$-modules as finitely generated modules over $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ and defines quotient and submodule closure on the finite type of indecomposable isomorphism classes.

What Lean proves here

Boundary coefficients

At most nine indecomposables

The quotient and submodule counts agree in the first and last four degrees; for at most nine indecomposables, these degrees exhaust the polynomial.

Uniserial modules

Nakayama algebras

If $c_i$ is the composition length of the projective cover of the $i$th simple module, both level polynomials equal $\prod_i(1+q+\cdots+q^{c_i})$.

Separated algebra

Radical square zero

The separated triangular algebra is representation-directed, and duality compares its two orientations.

Bruhat interval

Representation-directed

A reduced Auslander–Reiten word parametrizes quotient-closed and submodule-closed supports by the same principal Bruhat interval.
